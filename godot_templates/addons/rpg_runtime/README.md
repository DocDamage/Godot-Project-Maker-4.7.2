# rpg_runtime

Runtime addon copied into every project generated by rpggen. Classes use the `Rpg` prefix.
This folder is generated-owned: rpggen may rewrite it on regeneration. Licensed MIT-0 (see LICENSE);
the version is in `VERSION` and the history in `CHANGELOG.md`.

Autoloads, in order: `Events`, `GameState`, `SaveManager`, `SceneRouter`, `RpgSettings`, `RpgSmoke`.

## Dialogue conditions

`expr := term (("and"|"or") term)*`, `term := ["not"] atom`. Evaluated **left to right with no
precedence**, so `a or b and c` means `(a or b) and c`.

| Atom | True when |
| --- | --- |
| `flag:<entity>.<key>` | the flag is truthy |
| `item:<id>` / `item:<id>>=N` | the player holds at least 1 / N |
| `gold>=N` | the player has at least N gold |
| `level>=N` | the player's level is at least N |
| `quest:<id>=<state>` | the quest is in that state |
| `quest_active:<id>` / `quest_done:<id>` | the quest is under way / finished |
| `quest_stage:<id>:<stage>` | the quest is at that stage |
| `party:<actor id>` | the character is in the party (the player always is) |
| `var:<name><op><N>` | the variable compares so with N; `op` is `>=`, `<=`, `==`, `!=`, `>` or `<` (a variable never set is 0) |

## Dialogue events

`set_flag:<entity>.<key>=<value>`, `give_item:<id>[:qty]`, `take_item:<id>[:qty]`, `give_gold:N`,
`set_quest:<id>=<state>`, `quest_start:<id>`, `quest_stage:<id>:<stage>`, `quest_complete:<id>`,
`join_party:<actor id>` / `leave_party:<actor id>` (see below), `heal_full` (the whole party), `open_shop:<id>` (opens `game/data/shops/<id>.tres` when the
dialogue closes), `set_var:<name>=<N>`, `add_var:<name>:<N>` (N may be negative),
`run_sequence:<id>` (plays `game/data/sequences/<id>.tres` once the dialogue has closed),
`play_sound:<slot>` (a sound slot, your own included; silent when the slot has no sound) and
`emit:<name>[:arg]` (sent as `Events.dialogue_event`).

## Sequences

A sequence is a cutscene: an `RpgSequence` resource (`game/data/sequences/<id>.tres`) whose
`commands` are Dictionaries such as `{"cmd": "move_actor", "entity": "town_npc_001", "tiles": [2, 0]}`.
`entity` is an `entity_id` of the current map, or `player`; distances are in tiles of the map.
The full command list with every argument is at the top of `events/sequence.gd`; in short:

| Commands | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| `move_actor`, `face`, `animate` | walk a character (x first, then y; walls do not stop it), turn it, hold an animation |
| `wait`, `fade_out`, `fade_in`, `shake` | timing and the screen (the shake follows Settings > Screen shake) |
| `camera_pan`, `camera_lock`, `camera_unlock` | move the camera away from the player and back; keep it still |
| `show_dialogue`, `play_sound` | a dialogue file or lines written into the command; a sound slot |
| `give_item`, `take_item`, `set_flag`, `set_var`, `add_var`, `event` | change the game (`event` takes any dialogue event) |
| `start_battle`, `change_map` | a battle with enemies from `game/data/enemies/` (a defeat ends the sequence); another map |
| `label`, `goto`, `if` / `else`, `call`, `end` | flow: jump on a condition, run another sequence and come back |
| `wait_for` | wait for a character (or everything) started with `"wait": false` |

Characters, fades, pans and shakes started with `"wait": false` run in the background while the
next commands go on, so two characters can walk at once. While a sequence plays, the player
cannot move or open the pause menu, autosave waits and enemies hold still. When it ends,
the screen, the camera and every held animation are put back. A sequence marked `skippable` is
skipped with cancel. The same sequence always plays the same way (it advances with the physics
frames).

Start one with an `RpgTriggerArea` (dock: Project > *Add trigger area*): a walk-in or interact
area that runs `sequence_id` (or a sequence kept in the map in its `sequence` property), with
`once` remembered by the flag `<entity_id>.triggered` and an optional `condition`. The dialogue
event `run_sequence:<id>` and `RpgSequenceRunner.instance().run(sequence)` start one too; a second
one waits until the first has finished. The project validator reports unknown commands, missing
arguments and labels, and sequences, dialogues, enemies or maps that do not exist (VAL-023).

## Party members

`join_party:<id>` adds the character defined in `game/data/actors/<id>.tres` (an
`RpgActorDefinition`: name, sprite frames, portrait, starting stats); `leave_party:<id>` takes them
out again and returns what they wore to the inventory. The party holds at most 4 characters, the
player included. On the map the members walk behind the player, one tile apart along the player's
path (`RpgFollowers`, pictures without a body; boot's `followers` export turns them off). In battle every member picks a command in party order, ally-targeted commands
(items) can be aimed at any standing member, and experience is shared: each member who is standing
at the end gets the full amount. A member who fell is back on their feet with 1 HP when the battle
is over. The status menu pages through the members (left / right, or its first button), and each
has their own equipment. The party is part of the save.

In the **action RPG** the members follow in the same way, and the first two help in a fight
(`RpgAssist`): when an enemy is within 3 tiles of the player the follower walks beside it and
swings its own hitbox every 1.2 s with the member's attack. It stays within 6 tiles of the
player and walks back to its place when the enemy is gone or the player moves on. Enemies can
hit it, but it cannot die: at 0 HP it is down for 5 s and then back at full health. The HUD
shows a small HP bar per member under the player's.

## Equipment and shops

Items of the categories WEAPON, ARMOR and ACCESSORY are worn in the slot of that name
(`GameState.equip` / `unequip`, per actor; the old piece goes back to the inventory). Their
`modifiers` (`[{"stat": "attack", "add": 3}]`, optionally `"mul"`) change the stats both combat
systems read through `GameState.effective_stat`; `ranged: true` on a weapon makes the action RPG
shoot arrows. The save keeps the worn items (optional key `equipment`).

A shop is an `RpgShopDefinition` (`game/data/shops/<id>.tres`): `stock` is `[{item_id, price}]`
(a price below 0 means the item's `value`) and `sell_ratio` (default 0.5) is what it pays. The
dialogue event `open_shop:<id>` opens `shop_menu` when the dialogue closes; key and quest items are
never bought.

## Quests

An `RpgQuestDefinition` (`game/data/quests/<id>.tres`) has a `title` key, ordered `stages` and
`rewards` (`gold`, `experience`, `items`). A stage is `{id, description, complete_when, target}`:
`complete_when` is a dialogue condition (when it holds the quest moves on by itself, and the last
stage completes it and gives the rewards once); `target`, optional, is the entity id the minimap
marks. Also optional: `next` (the stage that follows instead of the one below, or `done` to
finish the quest there), `rewards` (given when the stage's condition finishes it) and `title`.
`problems()` lists what is wrong with a quest and `reached(state)` says where it gets to.
`GameState.quests[id]` is the current stage id or `"done"`. Events and conditions are in the
tables above; the pause menu's Journal lists active and finished quests.

## Keys, switches and gates

`RpgGate` blocks the way until it opens: for good with its `key_item` (flag
`<entity_id>.unlocked`), or while its `open_when` condition holds. `RpgSwitch` is a floor plate
(latching, or `hold`) or a lever (`once` optional) that sets `<entity_id>.on`. A map door
(`RpgDoor`) can need a `key_item` too, and `consume_key` takes it. The dock's Project tab adds
gates and switches.

## Light, the minimap and the world map

Every `RpgMapRoot` gets a `CanvasModulate` named `Ambient`: daylight outdoors, dimmer when
`is_interior` (or the map id starts with `interior`), or `ambient_override`. Objects tagged `light`
get an `RpgLightSource` (a `PointLight2D` that flickers for a flame and switches itself off outside
the camera). With `RpgSettings.day_night` on, outdoor maps follow the play time (`RpgDayNight`).

Boot adds the HUD minimap (`RpgMinimap`, hidden by `RpgSettings.minimap`): the picture the build
made of each map in `generated/minimaps/`, the player, doors, unopened chests and quest targets,
and the cells not seen yet darkened. What the player has seen is saved per map (optional key
`map_fog`); the pause menu's Map (`RpgWorldMapMenu`) shows every visited map with the rest covered.

## Sound

`RpgAudio` (a child of `SceneRouter`, its `audio` property) plays the **sound slots** of
the game. `rpggen/sound_bank.json` maps a slot key (`music_map`, `music_battle`, `music_title`,
`sfx_ui_move`, `sfx_ui_confirm`, `sfx_ui_cancel`, `sfx_hit`, `sfx_attack`, `sfx_pickup`,
`sfx_chest`, `sfx_door`, `sfx_step`, `sfx_save`, `sfx_levelup`, or any slot of your own named
`sfx_<name>` or `music_<name>`) to a resource path. `play(slot)`,
`play_ui_slot(slot)` and `play_music_slot(slot)` do nothing, and log nothing, for a slot that is
not set. A slot named `sfx_step_<surface>` plays instead of `sfx_step` on ground of that
surface (`rpggen/surfaces.json`: asset id → surface). A track that loops does so between the
points of `rpggen/audio_loops.json` (`RpgAudio.apply_loop`). Game events (`item_added`, `player_damaged`, `level_up`, `game_saved`, `chest_opened`,
`door_used`, `attack_started`) are wired to their slots; the autosave (slot 0) is silent.

## Saving and game over

`SaveManager` has slots 1 to 3 and the autosave slot 0 (`AUTOSAVE_SLOT`); `latest_save_slot()` of
`RpgDefeatPolicy` is the newest of them, or -1. `RpgDefeatPolicy.show_game_over()` shows
`RpgGameOver` (continue, try again, quit to title) and carries the choice out; smoke runs and a
combat mode with an `auto_choose` hook skip the screen and take the default (the newest save,
else the start of the map with full health).

## Credits

`RpgCredits` (`data/credits.gd`) is the data of the Credits screen: `packs` (one
`{name, author, license, license_url, url, attribution_required}` per asset pack) and `extra`
(`{section, text}` rows). The builder's `credits_recipe` writes it to
`generated/data/credits.tres` on every build and seeds `game/data/credits_extra.tsv` once.
`extra_lines()` reads the .tsv when the project has it and the baked rows otherwise.
`RpgCreditsMenu` (`ui/credits_menu.gd`, scene `generated/scenes/ui/credits_menu.tscn`) lists your
lines by section, the packs and the engine; up and down scroll, Back or `cancel` closes it. The
title screen and the pause menu open it from their *Credits* button.

## Languages

Each column of `game/i18n/strings.tsv` becomes a Translation in `generated/i18n/`, registered at
boot by `RpgStrings.register_translations()`. `RpgSettings.set_language("fr")` switches the game
(`""` follows the system); `RpgStrings.locales()` lists what can be chosen. A label whose text is a
key follows a new language by itself; text set in code with `tr()` has to be set again: listen to
`NOTIFICATION_TRANSLATION_CHANGED` (or `RpgSettings.changed`), as the settings menu, the title
screen and the battle interface do.

## Input glyphs

`RpgInputGlyphs.glyph("interact")` is `"[E]"` while the player uses the keyboard and `"[A]"`,
`"[Cross]"` or `"[B]"` once they press a button on an Xbox, PlayStation or Nintendo pad (a pad of
unknown make shows `"[Pad 0]"`). It reads the first binding of that kind of device from the
InputMap each time, so remapped keys are right. `RpgInputGlyphs.hint([["confirm", "Confirm"],
["cancel", "Back"]])` makes a line of hints; actions the project does not define are left out.
Connect to `RpgSettings.input_device_changed` to redraw a prompt when the device changes (the
battle interface and the title screen do). The glyphs are text, so they use the project font.

## Debug overlay

In debug builds with the debug tools feature on, **F3** shows FPS, map, position, tile and flag
count, and a console: `tp <spawn_id>`, `give <item_id> [qty]`, `gold <n>`, `flag <entity>.<key>=<v>`,
`heal`, `encounter`, `collision`, `help`.

## Archetypes

Each archetype lives in `archetypes/<name>/`; a generated project contains only the one it was
generated with. The shared code never refers to an archetype's classes: boot adds the archetype's
combat mode (`SceneRouter.combat`, an `RpgCombatMode`) and shared code only calls its contract.

### turn_based

An overworld enemy (`RpgOverworldEnemy`, a Contact area on layer `triggers`) builds an
`RpgEncounterData` and calls `SceneRouter.combat.start_encounter(data)`. `RpgTurnBasedMode` freezes
the player, pushes `generated/scenes/battle/battle.tscn` and, when the battle is over,
`SceneRouter.pop_scene(result)` brings the map back. Then the mode applies the result: VICTORY sets
the flag `<entity_id>.defeated` (so `RpgPersistent` frees the enemy on every map load), ESCAPED
disables the enemy's contact for 1.5 s, DEFEAT returns to the newest save, or to the map's default
spawn with full health when there is none.

The battle is `RpgBattleController`, a state machine
(`INIT > ROUND_START > PLAYER_CHOOSE > ENEMY_CHOOSE > EXECUTE > CHECK > VICTORY | DEFEAT | ESCAPED > END`)
that knows no nodes. Commands are `RpgBattleCommand` resources (`game/data/battle_commands/`):
`attack`, `defend` (priority 10, halves damage until the next round), `item`, `skill` and `run`.
Damage, hit, crit, magic, heal, element and escape formulas are pure functions in `RpgBattleMath`;
what an attack or a skill does to a battler (log entries) is `RpgBattleEffects`; the order of a
round is `RpgTurnManager.order` (priority, speed with haste and slow, player side, id; never
random). All randomness comes from `GameState.rng`, so a seeded battle is repeatable.

Skills are `RpgSkillDefinition` resources (`game/data/skills/<id>.tres`, loaded by the controller
into `skills`). The player side knows what its `RpgActorDefinition.skills` lists for its level and
pays `mp_cost` from `RpgStats.mp`; an enemy with `max_mp` 0 casts for free. Elements scale damage
by the target's rate (`RpgBattleActor.element_rate`: the definition and the worn items, the lowest
listed rate; 0 immune, negative absorbs). Statuses (`RpgBattleStatus.TABLE`) live in
`RpgBattleActor.statuses` (status -> rounds left, 0 = the whole battle) and end with the battle;
poison and regen tick and every status counts down after each round.

Enemy AI: the rules of `behavior.turn_based_rules` first (`RpgBattleAi`: the first rule whose
`when` holds and whose `chance` is rolled is used; `once` rules run once a battle), then a heal
item from `behavior.items` below 25% HP, else a roll of `behavior.turn_based`
(`{"attack": 1.0, "defend": 0.0}`; a key may be a skill id) at a random living player-side
battler. What it cannot do (a skill without the MP, nobody to aim at) becomes an attack.

Tests and the smoke run set `controller.auto_choose` (or `SceneRouter.combat.auto_choose` for the
next battle): it is called as `auto_choose.call(actor, battle)` and returns
`{command_id, target_index}` (plus `item_id` or `skill_id`); no UI is needed then.

Random encounters: an `RpgEncounterZone` (an `Area2D` whose `CollisionPolygon2D` /
`CollisionShape2D` children are its shape, or the painted cells of a TileMapLayer: `cells_layer`,
else a layer named `Encounters`, which is hidden in the game) points at an `RpgEncounterTable`
(`game/data/encounters/<id>.tres`: `groups` = [{enemies: [<enemy ids>], weight}], `can_escape`).
The mode counts the tiles the player walks (`RpgEncounterSteps`; a jump of more than two tiles is
a door, not walking) and at every step inside an enabled zone draws from `GameState.rng`: one in
`steps_per_encounter` steps brings a battle with a group drawn by weight. Every battle (random or
not) is followed by 6 grace steps without one, and nothing is counted during a map change, a
battle or a sequence. A zone is off while its `disabled_flag` (`<entity>.<key>`) is true. A random
battle defeats nothing on the map. The dock's Project tab has *Add encounter zone (over the
selection)*; the validator reports zones without a table, with unknown enemies or without a shape
(VAL-024). The action RPG has no random encounters: its enemies stand on the map (spawners are not
part of 1.0).

### Real-time combat (`combat/realtime/`)

`RpgDamageInfo`, `RpgHitbox`, `RpgHurtbox`, `RpgHealth`, `RpgProjectile`, `RpgStateMachine`,
`RpgPickup`, `RpgScreenFeedback`, `RpgRealtimeMath` and `RpgRealtimeText` are shared by the
archetypes that fight on the map (the action RPG and the metroidvania). Until 1.1 they were in
`archetypes/arpg/`; a script at each old path still extends the new one (removed in 2.0).

### arcade (shared by the shoot-em-up, the run and gun and the racing game)

A run through stages with lives and a score. `RpgArcadeMode` (`SceneRouter.combat`; the mode of
a kind extends it) owns the run: it binds every `RpgArcadeStage` the router enters
(`bind_stage`), counts kills (`RpgArcadeScore`: `add_kill`, `add_pickup`, `hit`; a multiplier up
to x8 that a hit resets) and lives (`RpgArcadeLives`: `lose`, an extra life every `extra_every`
points), shows the tally of a cleared stage (`RpgArcadeStageClear`), goes to the next stage and
ends the run with the high-score entry and the credits. `player_hit()`, `player_died()` and
`pickup(points)` are what a kind calls; `life_lost`, `game_over`, `continued`, `stage_started`,
`stage_cleared`, `stage_wanted` and `run_finished` are its signals. After the last life
`RpgArcadeContinueScreen` asks: Continue starts the stage again with the score kept.

`RpgArcadeStage` is an `RpgMapRoot` with a `Camera`, a `Scroller` (`RpgArcadeScroller`: `axis`
up, right or none, `speed`, `length`, `hold(who)` / `release(who)`, `progress`), a node
`Entities` and one `RpgArcadeSpawner` per wave of its entry in `game/data/stages.json`
(`RpgArcadeStageSequence`). A wave (`RpgArcadeWave`) is a formation (`line`, `column`, `v`,
`circle`, `sine`) with a trigger (`start`, `progress`, `time`, `after`) and may be `blocking`.
An enemy is any `Node2D`; with a signal `defeated` its kill is scored (its `points`, else the
wave's), and it is gone when it leaves the tree. A stage ends by `exit_condition`: `reach_end`,
`clear_waves` or `custom` (the kind calls `finish()`). Set `enemy_factory` and `boss_factory` to
build enemies in code. `RpgArcadeBoss` has health, `RpgArcadeBossPhase`s that change at a
fraction of it, `telegraphed` before `attacked`, an intro and a defeat with `exploded`; a kind
overrides `_move(pattern, delta, time_s)` and `_perform(attack)`.

`RpgArcadeHud` (`hud_arcade.tscn`) shows lives, score, multiplier, the stage and a boss's health;
`set_row(key, text)` adds a row of a kind. `RpgArcadeHighScores` keeps the ten best runs in the
shared section of the saves (`SaveManager.read_shared`, `shared_value`, `set_shared_value`:
`user://saves/meta.json`, one file for every slot). The game saves by itself when a stage is
entered; the pause menu's Save is off.

### run_and_gun (with `arcade` and the platform code of `metroidvania`)

`RpgSoldier` extends `RpgPlatformActor` with a gun that is aimed eight ways. It is told what to
want (`move_x`, `move_y`, `press_jump()`, `jump_held`, `shooting`, `aim_locked`,
`press_grenade()`) by `RpgSoldierInput` (the actions `jump`, `shoot`, `grenade`, `aim_lock` and
the four directions). `RpgGunAim.direction(stick, facing, on_floor, crouching)` is the aim: up,
the diagonals, forward, and down only in the air; down on the ground is a crouch, which lowers
`hit_center()` and `hit_radius` and fires forward. `RpgGun` (the child `Gun`: `rate`,
`bullets_per_shot`, `spread_deg`, `bullet_speed`, `range_px`, `damage`, `pierce`) fires into
the stage's bullet pool; the child `Muzzle` flashes where the shot goes. `hit()` costs one of
`max_health` (1 by default: a death); `die()`, `respawn()` (dropped in from the top of the
screen, safe for `invulnerable_s`). The soldier leads the camera through the stage's scroller
(`RpgArcadeScroller.follow`): to the right only. The left edge of the screen kills, or is a
wall (`left_edge_kills`); a fall out of the stage is a death. `RpgGrenade` flies an arc and
hurts every target within `blast_radius`. `RpgRunAndGunMode` takes a life per death and brings
the soldier back; `RpgRunAndGunHud` shows the grenades and the health of a forgiving soldier.

`RpgArcadeBulletPool` and `RpgArcadeExplosion` (in `arcade/`) are shared by the kinds that
shoot. The pool has `wall_mask` (the physics layers that stop a bullet: the ground of a
side-view stage), a `life_s` per bullet (the range of a gun), and hits a node at its
`hit_center()` when it has that method.

### shmup (the shoot-em-up; with `arcade`)

`RpgShmupMode` is the arcade mode with a ship and an `orientation` (`vertical` or `horizontal`);
`RpgShmupMath` says what follows from it (`forward_of`, `axis_of`, `play_area`, `start_offset`).
`RpgShmupShip` (a `CharacterBody2D`) flies inside its `play_area`, which rides with the camera;
it is told what to want (`move`, `shooting`, `focusing`, `press_shoot()`, `press_bomb()`) by
`RpgShmupShipInput` (the actions `shoot`, `bomb`, `focus` and the four directions). What can be
hit is one point (`hit_radius`); `hit()` destroys it, `respawn()` brings it back blinking for
`invulnerable_s`. Its children are `Visual`, `Weapon` (`RpgShmupWeapon`: `kind` straight, spread,
laser or homing, `level` 1 to 3, `rate`; `shots(kind, level)` says what one shot is), `Bomb`
(`RpgShmupBomb`: `use(pool, view)`) and `Input`.

`RpgArcadeBulletPool` (the node `Bullets` of a stage) holds 400 bullets of the player and 1,000
of the enemies as numbers, not nodes: `fire(team, at, velocity, damage, radius, kind, homing,
pierce)`, `clear(team)`, `active(team)`. A player's bullet hits the nodes of the group
`arcade_targets` (a `hit_radius` and `take_hit(damage)`), an enemy's the ship. `RpgShmupEnemyBullet`
has the patterns `aimed`, `ring`, `fan` and `spiral` and `fire(pool, from, velocities)`.
Enemies (`enemies/`) extend `RpgShmupEnemy`: `health`, `points`, `hit_radius`, `take_hit`,
the signal `defeated`, a touch that destroys the ship, `drop` (the power-up it leaves) and
`in_stage` (it scrolls past with the background instead of flying with the screen). A behaviour
overrides `_fly`, `_drift`, `_act` and `_read_wave`. `RpgShmupPopcorn` flies a `path` and may
fire one aimed shot; the enemies of a formation fly the same path, so the formation moves as
one. `RpgShmupTurret` fires a fan while it is on the screen, `RpgShmupAsteroid` drifts and
breaks once into two `pieces()`, `RpgShmupCarrier` stays a third of the way in and releases
its `escort_scene`. `RpgShmupPowerup` drifts to the ship's side and is picked up by a touch;
`apply(kind, ship, mode)` is what it gives. The ship's `set_shield(true)` takes the next hit.

The framework's part of it: a spawned enemy with a method `spawned_in_wave(wave, index)` is
told its place; `RpgArcadeWave.data` is the whole entry of the wave and `bonus` what a formation
shot whole pays (`RpgArcadeSpawner.cleared_whole`, `RpgArcadeStage.formation_bonus`);
`RpgArcadeStage.adopt(enemy, at)` takes in an enemy no wave brought and scores its kill;
`RpgArcadeLives.gain()` is one more life.

`RpgShmupBoss` is the arcade boss as a target of the player's bullets: `take_hit`, a touch
that destroys the ship, and `volley(attack)` for the attacks `ring`, `fan`, `spiral`, `aimed`
and `sweep`, with the `count` and `fast` its phase has in the stage file. An enemy reads `fast`
(its bullets) and `tough` (its health) from its wave, a turret also `fan`.
`RpgArcadeMode.start_lives` is the lives of a run; `planned_stages_path` names the generator's
own list of stages, from which the mode takes what `stages_path` does not list
(`RpgArcadeStageSequence.merged`).

`RpgShmupHud` has `panels`: in a game that scrolls up the HUD goes into the strips beside the
play area (`lay_out_panels(view)`, `panel_width()`), the high score, the stage, the bombs and
the weapon on the left, the score and the lives on the right. The title screen asks the combat
mode for entries of its own (`customize_title_screen(title)`); `RpgArcadeMode` adds *High
scores* (`show_high_scores(over, back)`). The ship plays `shoot_sound` with every shot.

`RpgArcadeExplosion` plays once and is gone; `RpgShmupStarfield` draws the stars of a stage whose
packs have no background; `RpgShmupHud` adds the bombs and the weapon to the arcade HUD.

### metroidvania

The game is seen from the side. `RpgMetroidvaniaMode` (`SceneRouter.combat`) never has an
encounter; it owns the rules of saving (`save_at_stations`, `autosave_on_every_map`) and what
happens after a defeat (`respawn_after_defeat()`: the last save station used, full health,
nothing lost).

`RpgPlatformActor` is moved by **intents**, not by reading the input itself: `move_x`, `move_y`,
`jump_held`, `press_jump()`, `release_jump()`, `press_dash()`, then `step(delta)`.
`RpgPlatformInput.poll(actor)` turns the input actions into intents for the player; an enemy or
a test sets them directly. Tuning is in tiles and seconds (`jump_height_tiles`, `jump_time_to_peak`,
`coyote_s`, `jump_buffer_s`, …), so it holds for any tile size. One-way platforms are tiles on
physics layer 11 (`RpgSideTiles.ONE_WAY_LAYER`); ladders, hazards and breakable blocks are
custom data of the tiles. Animations have no facing (`idle`, `run`, `jump`, `fall`, `attack`,
`dash`, `climb`, `hurt`): `RpgAnimationDriver.play_side` mirrors them.

Abilities are ids in `GameState.abilities` (`RpgAbilities.ORDER`, `TABLE`). `RpgAbilityPickup`
grants one and removes itself for good; `RpgAbilityGate` and `RpgGate.required_ability` ask for
one; in conditions it is `ability:<id>`.

A room is an `RpgRoom` (`game/maps/rooms/<id>.tscn`): `Background`, the tile layers, `Entities`,
`Triggers` (an `RpgRoomExit` per way out, with `target_room` and `target_exit`), `SpawnPoints`
(one per exit, same name) and `Camera` (`RpgSideCamera`, limited to the room).
`SceneRouter.go_to_room(map, exit_id, carry)` changes the room without a fade; `RpgRoom.arrive`
places the player at the exit with the speed, facing and height it left with, and the camera
slides in. The plan of the rooms is `rpggen/world_plan.json` (`rooms`).

Enemies are `RpgSideEnemy` with one behaviour script (`patrol`, `flyer`, `shooter`); the
`RpgGuardian` has three phases by its health, telegraphs its attacks, grants an ability when it
dies and opens the gates of its room (`<room>_guardian.defeated`). `RpgSaveStation.rest()` heals,
clears the `defeated` flag of ordinary enemies and remembers the place to return to.
`RpgMetroidvaniaHud` is the HUD scene `hud_metroidvania.tscn`.

### arpg

Combat happens on the map. `RpgArpgMode` (`SceneRouter.combat`) never has an encounter
(`is_in_combat()` is false); it owns the defeat policy (`apply_defeat()`, the same as the
turn-based one), the quick item (`quick_item()`, `set_quick_item(id)`, `use_quick_item()`; the
choice is the flag `arpg_hud.quick_item`) and the "Set quick item" button it adds to the inventory
menu (`customize_inventory_menu(menu)`).

Damage primitives: `RpgHitbox` (an `Area2D` that is live only while `active`; hits each hurtbox once
per activation, polls overlaps because a hurtbox that is already inside gives no `area_entered`),
`RpgHurtbox` (`receive(info)`, an invulnerability window: player 0.6 s, enemies 0.15 s),
`RpgHealth` (the numbers live in the actor's `RpgStats`; `died` fires once) and `RpgDamageInfo`.
Layers: player hitbox 7 (mask 10), enemy hitbox 8 (mask 9), player hurtbox 9, enemy hurtbox 10.
`RpgArpgMath`: `max(1, attack - defense / 2)`, x1.5 on a crit, one `GameState.rng` draw per roll.

Animations are named `<action>_<facing>`. A facing is down, up, left or right; a sprite can also
have the diagonals `down_left`, `down_right`, `up_left` and `up_right`. `RpgAnimationDriver.play(
base, facing, dir)` plays the diagonal that `dir` (the direction of travel) points along when the
sprite has it, mirrors a missing left or right side, and otherwise falls back to the direction
45 degrees next to the facing, then to `down`. `RpgActor.facing` itself stays one of four.

`RpgPlayerCombat` is the player's state machine (`IDLE`, `MOVE`, `ATTACK`, `HURT`, `DODGE`,
`DEAD`; a generic `RpgStateMachine`). It uses the actor's `move_locked`, `facing_locked` and
`animation_locked` flags so the input and the automatic walk / idle animations do not fight it.
The hitbox is live on the active frames of `attack_<facing>` (SpriteFrames meta
`active_frames_<anim>`, else the middle third); without an attack animation `RpgSwingEffect` plays
the generated arc and the hitbox is live 0.08 to 0.2 s after the press.

`RpgEnemyAi` is only the state machine of an enemy (`IDLE`, `WANDER`, `ALERT`, `CHASE`, `ATTACK`,
`HURT`, `STUNNED`, `DEAD`); `Detection`, `Chase`, `Wander`, `EnemyAttack`, `EnemyShoot`,
`EnemyCharge` and `LootDrop` are components under `Components` (the shooting and charging ones are
made in `_ready` when the kind needs them). Their numbers are the keys of `definition.behavior`
(see `RpgEnemyAi.DEFAULT_BEHAVIOR`); `behavior.kind` is `melee`, `shooter` (keeps `keep_away_px`,
fires from `shoot_range_px`), `turret` (never moves) or `charger` (dashes with its hitbox live,
then recovers).

`RpgProjectile` is a shot made in code (`create(shooter, from, direction, team, speed, range)`): the
layers of its team's hitbox plus the world, the melee damage formula with the shooter's attack when
it was fired, gone after one hit, a wall or its range. A worn weapon with `RpgItemDefinition.ranged`
makes the player's attack fire one arrow (`RpgPlayerCombat.fire_arrow`) instead of the swing. A dead enemy sets the flag `<entity_id>.defeated` (an
`RpgPersistent` child frees it on every map load), emits `entity_defeated`, gives experience and gold
and drops `RpgPickup`s. `RpgArpgHud` is the HUD scene `hud_arpg.tscn`.
